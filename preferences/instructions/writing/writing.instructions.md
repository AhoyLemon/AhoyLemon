# Writing Preferences

Applies when writing *as Lemon* — email, blog posts, Slack messages, meeting notes, Notion prose, and anything else where words are the deliverable. Not for code comments or technical documentation, and not for Claude's own responses or explanations. Use whatever language and punctuation is natural when talking *to* Lemon.

## Voice & tone

- Direct, conversational, witty and occasionally off-kilter — never corporate or formal
- Self-deprecating humor is welcome. Don't take the writing too seriously.
- Clumsier and more natural beats smooth and polished — if two phrasings are equal, prefer the one that sounds more human
- Clarity over verbosity: say what you mean in the fewest words that still sound like you. Don't dumb down vocabulary — a well-placed word beats a circumlocution. The goal is clarity, not simplicity.

## Comedy & joke writing

For anything meant to be funny: bits, satire, parody, fake headlines, a bot's worth of jokes.

- **Go too far.** Lemon would rather pull a joke back than coax a timid one forward. Draft past the line and let the edit find where it should sit.
- **Specific beats generic.** "A synthwave DJ with the moniker The Inhuman" beats "a musician." If a line could describe anybody, it isn't done yet. Real brands count as specificity ("a brand advocate for Malibu" beats "a brand advocate for a rum company"), as long as the joke isn't a made-up claim about what the company does.
- **Let people tell on themselves.** It's funnier for a character to reveal their own flaws than for an outside narrator to point them out.
- **Twist the format now and then.** If you're writing a number of jokes with the same joke structure, try one where you subvert the format itself. The results can be surprisingly effective.

## Grammar (hard rules)

- **No em dashes (—).** Use a period, a comma, or restructure the sentence.
- **No semicolons (;).** Same fix: split into two sentences or use a comma.

## AI "tells" to avoid

Avoid these even if the output sounds slightly less polished as a result.

**Filler affirmations**
"Certainly!", "Absolutely!", "Of course!", "Great question!" — just answer the thing.

**Performative empathy**
"I understand this must be difficult", "I appreciate your patience" — if empathy is called for, say something real.

**Unnecessary hedges**
"it's worth noting that", "it's important to remember", "it should be noted" — cut the preamble, say the thing.

**Transition clichés**
"In conclusion", "To summarize", "In summary", "With that in mind" — restructure or just stop.

**Overused AI power words**
"delve", "leverage", "utilize" (just say "use"), "robust", "seamless", "streamlined", "comprehensive", "foster", "ensure" — reach for a more specific word, or a plainer one.

**The compliment sandwich**
Don't open with praise before delivering a message, and don't close with reassurance after it.

**Bullet-everything**
Lists are for genuinely list-shaped content. If it flows naturally as prose, write it as prose.

**Signposting filler**
"I'm going to walk you through...", "Let me explain..." — just explain it.

## Handling asks and requests

When Lemon asks for help writing something, there's almost always a core ask inside it — something he wants the reader to understand, agree to, or do. That's the load-bearing part of any draft.

- Identify the ask first, before worrying about tone or framing
- Never let elegant writing water down or obscure the actual request
- The ask should land clearly and feel natural — not gross, pushy, or AI-generated
- Frame it so it reads as confident but not demanding, clear but not blunt
